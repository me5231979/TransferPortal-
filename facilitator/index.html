05 · When things go sideways

If it breaks, you have a move

If Projector or the site fails mid-session: Teach from the printed quick reference: the guarantees, the six entrances, the two gears of the process, and the manager script all work on a whiteboard. The pair activities never needed a screen; the take-home moves to the printed job aid.
If Learner devices can't join (wifi or filters): Run facilitator-only: the trainers play well on the projector with the room voting A/B/C by hands, and the capstone moves to paper. You lose typing, not learning.
If You're running behind by the culture section (04): Declare the squeeze silently: remaining trainers become single room votes. Never cut Section 04's trainer, the recap, or the capstone; they carry the program's success.
If Someone shares a live, painful situation, a role elimination or a conflict with their manager: Honor it and bound it: 'That's real, and you deserve a better answer than a training room can give. Entrance E and the employee-consultant conversation exist for exactly this; let's connect after.' Follow up privately with the People, Culture and Belonging contact you prepped. Keep names off the whiteboard.
If A manager pushes back hard: 'so HR built a tool to raid my team': Don't argue; go to evidence. Ask them what it costs to backfill an external departure, then show the retention numbers and the hoarding research: the choice is between losing people to another Vanderbilt unit or to another employer. Then land the backfill cascade: their next great hire comes through the same portal.
If The room distrusts the privacy claims ('HR always says that'): Slow down and be concrete: interest declarations trigger no notifications, data reaches leaders only aggregated through Workforce Intelligence, and asking HR to work around that is itself a violation managers are trained on. Then write the residual doubts on the open-questions board and commit to written answers; skeptics convert on follow-through, not reassurance.

06 · Tough questions, ready answers

Say each answer out loud once before you teach

So my manager can see everything I do in there?

No. Your browsing, your Careers of Interest, and your skills profile are private to you. Your manager gets no notification when you declare interest, and leader-facing Workforce Intelligence shows aggregated, privacy-preserving data, never a list of who is looking. Your manager learns you applied for a role when you tell them or when the hiring process formally requires it, and the norm is that you have that conversation yourself once you are a serious candidate.

Isn't this just poaching with extra steps?

Poaching is secret and zero-sum. This is the opposite on both counts: the marketplace is open to every unit, the releasing manager gets a planned handoff and a backfill through the same system, and the alternative to an internal move is usually an external resignation, which has no handoff and no backfill window. The research backs it: about 75% of managers admit to talent hoarding, and hoarding deters internal applications without stopping external ones (Haegele, American Economic Review, 2024).

If I take a Gig and do great work, why doesn't that guarantee me the permanent role?

Because the Gig is development and the requisition is a competition, and keeping them separate protects you both ways. If Gigs converted automatically, every Gig would become a quiet audition and managers would guard them like headcount. Kept separate, you can stretch without betting your reputation, and when the real requisition opens, your Gig gives you evidence, relationships, and a warm start most candidates won't have.

What if my manager retaliates anyway?

Then the program's integrity rules are on your side: blocking an application, downgrading a rating in response, or excluding someone from key work are named as serious violations, and managers are trained on that before the portal reaches them. Document what happened and bring it to your HR business partner or an employee consultant. And a candid note: the guardrails only bite because leadership enforces them, which is exactly why manager training like this session exists.

Why would I tell the truth about my skill levels? Inflating them gets me matched to better roles.

It gets you matched to interviews you then lose. The hiring manager verifies skills in the interview, and an inflated profile spends your credibility exactly where you need it most. Honest levels do something better: they route you to roles and Gigs where you'll genuinely compete, and they turn the gaps into Grow goals instead of surprises.

We're short-staffed already. How is my unit supposed to survive its best people leaving?

Two honest answers. First, the counterfactual: your best people already have a marketplace; it's called every other employer, and it offers no handoff, no knowledge transfer, and no internal-first backfill. The portal keeps that talent at Vanderbilt and gives you two to four weeks of coordinated transition plus first look at internal candidates for the backfill. Second, the pattern compounds: units known for developing and releasing people attract the strongest internal applicants, so the pipeline runs both directions.

Section 01 · What It Is

One private portal for every internal move.

The Talent Transfer Portal is more than one screen. It is a program built on three pieces that work together.

  1. A conversation with your managerAn open, ongoing talk about where you want to go and the support you need to get there.
  2. A skill map you ownThe skills you have, the skills you want to grow, and your Careers of Interest. Private to you, always.
  3. Oracle, where it runsFour places inside Oracle: your Talent Profile, the Grow area, Oracle Learning, and the Opportunity Marketplace. Job postings appear there for Vanderbilt staff only for the first five business days, and Gigs will live there once they launch. Leaders see only group-level numbers through Workforce Intelligence, never your activity.
You control

What you share

Your profile, your Careers of Interest, your applications, and when you tell your manager. Showing interest alerts no one.

HR guarantees

Privacy by default

No alerts to your manager and no forced career talk. Staff see new postings first for five business days. Advisor help only when you ask.

Managers commit

A fair marketplace

No retaliation, no watching your activity, real coaching talks, respectful handoffs, and help refilling the role you leave.

Section 02 · Entrances

Match your situation to its entrance.

Six doors, six common situations. Yours might be a mix. Every door starts best the same way: a growth conversation with your manager. That talk is recommended, never required, and exploring stays private until you say otherwise. Open a door to see the path.

A · The Explorer "I'm curious, but not ready to move"

You right now Happy enough, curious what else is out there, and worried that curiosity looks like quitting.

  1. Growth conversation with your manager recommended start
  2. Update the skills in your Talent Profile
  3. Flag Careers of Interest in the Grow area
  4. Browse the Opportunity Marketplace at your own pace
  5. Suggestions arrive as matching roles open

Where it leads A private map of your options that grows while you do your job.

Worth knowing Flagging interest alerts no one. You choose if and when to say more.

B · The Tenured High Performer "I need a new challenge"

You right now Years of know-how, great reviews, and work that now runs on autopilot.

  1. Growth conversation with your manager recommended start
  2. Browse Gigs in the Opportunity Marketplace at launch
  3. Take one, with your manager's approval, scope and duration defined, home role intact
  4. Add the new skills to your Talent Profile
  5. Compete when the right requisition opens

Where it leads Stretch and fresh relationships now, and a stronger candidacy for the bigger next chapter.

Worth knowing Gigs have not launched yet. Until then, start with the conversation, your skills, and Careers of Interest. A Gig builds skills; it never promises a transfer. Real moves go through open job postings.

C · The Underutilized Contributor "I have skills I never get to use"

You right now Hired for one job, grew into more, and the extra skills sit unused.

  1. Growth conversation with your manager recommended start
  2. Tag every real skill at its true level in the Skills Center
  3. Point your Grow plan at the underused ones
  4. The portal surfaces roles and Gigs that need them
  5. Apply when the fit is real, or take a Gig once Gigs launch

Where it leads Work that finally uses the whole toolkit, found by matching instead of luck.

Worth knowing Matching runs on your skills data. Honest levels beat long lists.

D · The Post-Reorg Realigner "My role feels misaligned"

You right now Your area was reorganized, and the job you signed up for changed under you.

  1. Growth conversation with your manager recommended start
  2. Request an employee-consultant conversation, confidential and yours to open
  3. Sort out the next chapter with your advisor
  4. Apply competitively to the requisitions that fit
  5. Transition through a coordinated handoff

Where it leads Clarity about the next chapter before you commit to chasing it.

Worth knowing You start the advisor talk, never HR, and nothing happens without your OK.

E · The At-Risk Employee "I've heard my role may be at risk"

You right now A restructure, a cut, or budget pressure may hit your role.

  1. Growth conversation with your manager recommended start
  2. Ask for advisory support and priority matching
  3. Your skills are matched proactively against open needs
  4. Faster review where your skills fit
  5. Land the next role before the risk lands

Where it leads The program working hardest exactly when you need it most.

Worth knowing Advisor talks stay private. Priority matching starts only when you ask.

F · The Direct Applicant "There's an open job I want"

You right now A posting is live, your skills match, and you are ready now.

  1. Growth conversation with your manager recommended start
  2. Apply inside the five-business-day internal-first window
  3. Interview in the same process every candidate gets
  4. HR and both managers plan the handoff, typically two to four weeks
  5. Onboard fast while your old role backfills through the portal

Where it leads A new chapter at Vanderbilt with your institutional knowledge riding along.

Worth knowing The five-day window gives you the first look. The interview is still yours to win.

Section 03 · The Process

Always be growing. Compete when a role opens.

Growth here is a conversation, never a surprise. Keep growing every day, and keep your manager in the loop. The portal has two gears: build day to day, then compete when a role you want opens. Each list runs in order.

Every day · Growing and developing
  1. Keep the growth conversation going. Your manager should hear your goals from you.
  2. Build your profile. In your Talent Profile (Oracle lists it as Skills and Qualifications): Career Preferences plus both skills lists, honestly leveled in the Skills Center. Private to you.
  3. Set a Grow plan and flag Careers of Interest. Both live in Grow: one to three goals, plus the roles you want suggestions about.
  4. Take the learning. Courses in Oracle Learning, tied to the skills you want to grow.
  5. Explore. Browse the Opportunity Marketplace with the Opportunity filter set to Career Roles: you are browsing careers, with or without an open requisition. Save what interests you.
  6. Take a Gig, once Gigs launch. A short project with a set scope and end date. Your manager approves it first, so it is never a secret. It builds skills; it never promises a transfer.
  7. Ask for advisor help any time. Private, your choice, and nothing happens without your OK.
When a role opens · The competitive move
  1. A job posts, for Vanderbilt staff only for the first five business days.
  2. You apply. Recruiters run one fair process, and the hiring manager sees what you choose to share.
  3. You interview, the same way every candidate does.
  4. If you win it, HR and both managers plan the handoff, usually two to four weeks.
  5. You start fast; you already know Vanderbilt.
  6. Your old job posts the same way, so your team gets the same first look.

And applying from inside never means the job is yours; the window gives you the first look, and the interview is yours to win.

Section 04 · The Culture Shift

Releasing talent is a leadership win.

Some managers hear "internal mobility" and think "poaching." Research measured it: about three out of four managers admit to holding on to strong team members (talent hoarding), and that pressure stops people from applying inside (Haegele, American Economic Review, 2024). Left alone, hoarding starves the portal. The culture work is the work.

The reframe every manager needs

When a team member grows into a new role at Vanderbilt, that is a win on your record: we keep someone we would have lost, their skills land where the university needs them, and you get to bring fresh talent onto your team.

The response that builds the culture

"Thank you for telling me. Tell me what drew you to it. What can I do to help you compete well, and whatever the outcome, what should we plan for next in your development here?"

The response the culture cannot survive

"After everything I've invested in you?"

The first response builds a career at Vanderbilt. The second builds a resume for somewhere else.

Manager guardrails

No retaliation: blocking an application, lowering a rating, or sidelining an applicant is a serious violation. No watching: no alerts, no workarounds. Do coach: have the "what's next for you here" talk before a job posting has it for you.

Score what stuck (Level 2), then hand over the runway: five small moves with the exact Oracle screens and live links.

Say

“Six quick questions first; this is the check before the take-home. Then the job aid: five small moves, each on a real Oracle screen, with live links that open them. Move one is the growth conversation. Print it from the button right here.”

Do
  1. Run the recap on devices; hands for 5 or better, and a thirty-second reteach on anything the room missed.
  2. Walk the five moves left to right; point at the screenshots and the live links.
  3. Name move one as the through-line: the growth conversation.
  4. Point at the Print the job aid button for the take-home.

Validate: Room mode at 5/6 or better on the quiz; every learner can name their first move.

Watch for: Turning this into a planning exercise; the course educates, the moves come later.
