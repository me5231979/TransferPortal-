Section 01 · What It Is

Your career, in one place.

One front door for internal movement: requisitions with a two-week internal-first window, Careers of Interest, your skills, Grow plans, and Gigs (short stretch assignments). In Oracle: your Talent Profile, the Grow area, and the Opportunity Marketplace. Leaders see only aggregated data, through Workforce Intelligence.

You control

What you share

Your profile, your Careers of Interest, your applications, and when you tell your manager. Declaring interest notifies no one.

HR guarantees

Privacy by default

No auto-notification to your manager. Two weeks of internal-first posting. Advisory support only when you ask.

Managers commit

A fair marketplace

No retaliation, no surveillance, coaching conversations, respectful handoffs, and partnership on the backfill.

Go deeper

In practice

The clean way to hold it: a competitive internal marketplace, and the word that matters is yours. Gigs are optional stretch work; permanent moves run through real requisitions; interest data rolls up only in aggregate; and nothing HR-initiated happens to you through it.

As a group · optional extension

Why this matters: naming your worries out loud, then checking them against the guarantees, is the fastest way to find out which fears are real and which are folklore.

  1. In pairs: each name the one thing you'd be most nervous about if you used the portal tomorrow.
  2. Check each worry against the guarantee cards above. Covered, or genuinely open?
  3. Bring the genuinely open ones to the room; the facilitator collects them for HR.

Flying solo? Write down your one worry, then find the guarantee that covers it. If none does, that's a real question worth sending to People, Culture and Belonging.

Section 02 · Entrances

Six entrances, one portal.

Six doors for six situations. Find yours today; change lanes any time.

A · The Explorer

"I'm curious, but not ready to move"

Generally happy, wondering what else exists here.

Enter through: Careers of Interest + skills profile. Browse and declare privately; suggestions start arriving. Your manager is not notified.

B · The Tenured High Performer

"I need a new challenge"

Deep institutional knowledge, plateaued in the current role.

Enter through: Gigs + Careers of Interest. A stretch assignment in another unit while keeping your home role. Development, with no transfer promise.

C · The Underutilized Contributor

"I have skills I never get to use"

Hired for one thing, grew into others, no outlet.

Enter through: skills update + requisition search. Tag skills at their true level; the portal surfaces roles and Gigs that need them.

D · The Post-Reorg Realigner

"My role feels misaligned"

Team, mission, or manager just changed materially.

Enter through: advisory conversation + requisitions. An advisor helps you clarify what's next; you apply competitively. You start it, never HR.

E · The At-Risk Employee

"I've heard my role may be at risk"

Restructure, elimination, or budget pressure looming.

Enter through: advisory + priority matching. Proactive support and expedited review where your skills transfer.

F · The Direct Applicant

"There's an open job I want"

Knows the role, knows they're ready.

Enter through: the requisition listing. Apply in the two-week window, interview, and if selected, transition with a coordinated handoff.

Play · Pick the entrance

Why this matters: the doors only help if you can match them to real situations. Five colleagues; pick each one's door.

Go deeper

In practice

Entrances are situations, never categories; most careers use two or three of them. The advisory doors (D and E) are confidential and yours to open, and E adds priority matching and expedited review: the program working hardest exactly when someone needs it most.

As a group · optional extension

Why this matters: claiming an entrance out loud turns an org chart into a personal plan, and hearing the room's spread proves every door gets used.

  1. Everyone privately picks the entrance closest to their situation today.
  2. Show of hands, A through F. Notice the spread; every room has all six.
  3. Pairs: tell your partner which you picked and what the first click would be.

Flying solo? Write down your entrance letter and the first thing you'd do inside it. Keep it; the capstone will ask for exactly this.

Section 03 · The Process

Two tracks, both yours.

Interest & Development is always on and private. Move starts when a requisition opens, and it is competitive. Same portal; they feed each other.

Track 1 · Interest & Development, always on
  1. Build your profile. In your Talent Profile: Career Preferences plus both skills lists, honestly leveled. Private to you.
  2. Set a Grow plan and flag Careers of Interest. Both live in Grow: one to three goals, plus the roles you want suggestions about.
  3. Explore. Browse the Opportunity Marketplace; save what interests you.
  4. Take a Gig, optionally. Defined scope, defined duration, real development. No transfer guarantee.
  5. Ask for advisory support, optionally. Confidential, on your initiative; nothing happens without your consent.
Track 2 · Move, event-driven
  1. A requisition opens, internal-first for two weeks.
  2. You apply. The hiring manager sees what you choose to share; performance data follows policy.
  3. You interview, in the same process every candidate gets.
  4. Transition is coordinated. HR and both managers plan the handoff, typically two to four weeks.
  5. You onboard fast; your institutional knowledge comes with you.
  6. Your old role backfills through the same portal.

Play · Call the next move

Why this matters: the process feels safe when every next step is predictable. Five moments; call what happens.

Go deeper

In practice

The disclosure rule, straight: you owe nobody an announcement for exploring. Once you're a serious candidate, have the professional conversation with your manager before a workflow does it for you. And internal application never equals entitlement: the window gives you first look; the interview is yours to win.

As a group · optional extension

Why this matters: the two-track model clicks when you trace one imaginary colleague all the way through it.

  1. Pairs: invent a colleague (role, not a real name) and pick their entrance from Section 02.
  2. Walk them through Track 1: what would they build, save, or ask for this month?
  3. Now a requisition opens that fits. Walk Track 2 to the handoff. Where would it wobble, and who fixes it?

Flying solo? Trace yourself instead: your Track 1 this month, and the Track 2 you'd want ready if the right posting appeared next quarter.

Section 04 · The Culture Shift

Let's talk about poaching.

Some managers will hear "internal mobility" and think "poaching." That reaction has been measured: about three quarters of managers admit to talent hoarding, and hoarding deters internal applications (Haegele, American Economic Review, 2024). Left alone, the average manager suppresses exactly what this portal needs. The culture work is the work.

The reframe every manager needs

When a team member grows into a new role at Vanderbilt, that is a win on your leadership record: retention we'd otherwise lose, skills landing where the university needs them, and a backfill that brings your team fresh capability.

The response that builds the culture

"Thank you for telling me. Tell me what drew you to it. What can I do to help you compete well, and whatever the outcome, what should we plan for next in your development here?"

The response the culture cannot survive

"After everything I've invested in you?"

The first response builds a career at Vanderbilt. The second builds a resume for somewhere else.

Manager guardrails

No retaliation: blocking, rating threats, or sidelining an applicant is a serious violation. No surveillance: no notifications, no workarounds. Do coach: hold the "what's next for you here" conversation before a requisition holds it for you.

Play · Judge the response

Why this matters: the program lives or dies in the sixty seconds after "I've applied for something." Five responses; call each one.

Go deeper

In practice

Hoarding is incentives, so the rollout counters structure with structure (Kotter's model): urgency from retention risk and vacancy costs, champion managers celebrated for releasing talent, the internal-first window and structured handoffs, early "first mover" stories, and a release rate on leader scorecards. Staff hold guardrails too: honest ratings, respectful competition, timely disclosure.

As a group · optional extension

Why this matters: drafting the words before the moment arrives is what makes the good response the one that actually comes out.

  1. Managers: draft the two sentences you'll say the next time a team member tells you they've applied. Staff: draft how you'd open the disclosure conversation with your manager.
  2. Pairs, one of each where possible: read them to each other. Does the manager line pass the "thank you first" test? Does the staff line land before the requisition would?
  3. Volunteers read the best lines to the room. Collect them; they become the unit's shared script.

Flying solo? Draft both lines anyway, whichever chair you sit in today. You will eventually sit in the other one.

Section 05 · The Evidence

The case, in numbers.

Built to be borrowed: every claim is cited and linked. First, test your instincts.

Play · Guess the number

Why this matters: a number you guessed at and missed sticks. Five findings; call each before the reveal.

Retention

70% vs 45%

Odds of staying for employees promoted within three years of hire, versus those who never moved. Lateral moves land at 62%. SHRM / LinkedIn, 32 million profiles.

Tenure

5.4 vs 2.9 years

Average tenure at high-mobility organizations versus low. Internal movers are 40% more likely to stay three years or more. Inop.ai benchmark synthesis; LinkedIn Global Talent Trends 2024.

Engagement

~60% higher

Engagement after an internal move; a 56-study meta-analysis (N = 284,086) ties rotation to satisfaction and lower burnout. Inop.ai; Mlekus & Maier.

Speed

20 to 25% faster

How much sooner internal hires reach full competency; internal-first hiring also cuts time-to-hire by roughly 10 to 20 days. LinkedIn Workplace Learning Report; Bersin Factbook.

Cost

Up to 3 to 5x

Full cost of hiring externally once time and lost productivity count; direct costs alone run 18 to 20% higher. Bersin; SHRM 2024 benchmarking.

Quality

Internal > external

Internal hires outperform external ones and stay longer; rotation-heavy firms are the innovative ones. Cornell ILR; Eriksson & Ortega.

Quick check: These outcomes are real, and they still won't happen at Vanderbilt automatically. Which finding explains why?

Go deeper

In practice

The one-sentence pitch: "The Transfer Portal is Vanderbilt's internal talent marketplace, a private, Oracle-powered experience where employees see opportunities first, signal interest safely, and compete for stretch work or new roles, while leaders see aggregated readiness through Workforce Intelligence." Two spares: 93% stay longer where careers get investment (LinkedIn), and industry internal-hire rates have slumped to ~24% from ~40% (AMS): a gap Vanderbilt gets to win in.

As a group · optional extension

Why this matters: you retain what you can say out loud, with a source attached, under mild social pressure.

  1. Pairs: one of you is a skeptical department head with sixty seconds. The other makes the case for the portal using at least two cited numbers.
  2. Swap roles with two different numbers.
  3. Room debrief: which single statistic moved your skeptic most? Hands for retention, cost, speed, or the hoarding warning.

Flying solo? Write your own sixty-second version with the two numbers you'd actually use, and keep it where you can find it before your next leadership meeting.
