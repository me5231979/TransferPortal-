What is the Talent Marketplace?

In college sports, a player enters the transfer portal to find the right fit. This is Vanderbilt's version for staff, with one difference: you never leave the team. It is your home for growth and development; build your skills, so that when an opportunity arises, you are ready to pursue it.

The path

  • Identify where and how you want to grow.
  • Talk with your manager and build a growth plan together.
  • Develop the skills through learning, careers of interest, and short-term opportunities.
  • Pursue the opportunity. When one arises, you will be ready.

The idea

Like college sports, with one difference.

A player enters the transfer portal to find a place to use their whole game and keep growing. Ours works the same way. Explore careers of interest or take on short-term opportunities; either way, you are developing your skills, and those skills stay at Vanderbilt.

You are the player

Your skills are your game. Keep them sharp and on the record in Oracle.

Your manager is your coach

Talk about where you want to grow, and train toward it together.

Every day is the season

Develop now, so that when your moment arises, you are ready.

What it is

Two ways to grow.

The marketplace lives in Oracle. Whichever path you take, the point is the same: developing skills that transfer anywhere at Vanderbilt.

1 · Careers of interest

The long game

Name the careers that draw you. The marketplace shows the skills they take and points you to learning that closes the gap. As your skills grow, so do your options; when the right opportunity arises, staff see it first. Private until you act.

2 · Short-term opportunities

The short game

Within Oracle you can find short-term opportunities in other units. With your manager's OK, apply and take part. You gain experience, you gain skills, and you help a team that needs you. Your home job stays yours.

The promises

You control what you share. Showing interest alerts no one. Leaders see only group totals, never your activity. Managers may not punish or spy. Advisor help starts only when you ask.

Why it matters

Opportunity, skill development, and skills that can move where Vanderbilt needs them. That is good for careers and good for the university.

Who it's for

Three kinds of talent.

The marketplace serves three situations, and most of us will live in each one at some point. Every path starts the same way: a growth conversation with your manager. Exploring stays private until you say otherwise.

1 · Looking to grow

"I want new skills and a bigger future here."

Name your careers of interest, build a plan with your manager, and develop toward it every day. When the right opportunity arises, you will be ready to pursue it.

2 · Not a fit, right mission

"This role isn't working, but I believe in this place."

You should not have to leave Vanderbilt to find the right seat. Talk with a career advisor, privately, and find where your skills serve the mission best.

3 · Hidden talent

"I'm great at my job, and I can offer more."

Your role does not use everything you can do. Tag your real skills, then lend them through short-term opportunities. The university gains your whole game, and your home job stays yours.
