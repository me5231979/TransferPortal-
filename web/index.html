Section 01 · What It Is

Your career, in one place.

The Transfer Portal brings internal career movement into a single Oracle-powered experience: open internal requisitions with a two-week internal-first window, Careers of Interest, your skills profile, Grow development plans, performance history, succession slates, and Gigs, which are short-term stretch assignments. In Oracle, you'll meet these as your Talent Profile (Career Preferences and Skills), the Grow area, and the Opportunity Marketplace. Leaders see the same underlying data through Workforce Intelligence, aggregated and privacy-preserving, built for workforce planning rather than watching individuals.

You control

What you share

Your profile, your Careers of Interest, your Gig and requisition applications, and when you tell your manager. Declaring interest notifies no one.

HR guarantees

Privacy by default

No auto-notification to your manager, a two-week internal-first window on eligible requisitions, and advisory support that activates only when you ask.

Managers commit

A fair marketplace

No retaliation, no surveillance, coaching conversations, respectful handoffs, and partnership on the backfill.

Go deeper

In practice

The clean way to hold it: the Transfer Portal is a competitive internal marketplace, and the word that matters is yours. It is a supported path with clear norms rather than a way around your manager. Gigs are optional stretch work, and permanent moves run through real requisitions. Interest declarations roll up to People, Culture and Belonging only in aggregate. And nothing HR-initiated happens to you through it: advisory support activates when you ask, never before.

Section 02 · Entrances

Six entrances, one portal.

People arrive at a career question from different places, so the portal has more than one door. Find the one that sounds like you today. You can change lanes any time.

A · The Explorer

"I'm curious, but not ready to move"

Generally happy, wondering what else exists here.

Enter through: Careers of Interest and your skills profile. You browse and declare privately; tailored role and learning suggestions start arriving. Your manager is not notified.

B · The Tenured High Performer

"I need a new challenge"

Deep institutional knowledge, plateaued in the current role.

Enter through: Gigs plus Careers of Interest. A short-term stretch assignment in another unit, while keeping your home role. Gigs count as development and never promise a permanent move.

C · The Underutilized Contributor

"I have skills I never get to use"

Hired for one thing, grew into others, no outlet.

Enter through: a skills profile update plus requisition search. Tag skills at their true level and the portal surfaces roles and Gigs that need them.

D · The Post-Reorg Realigner

"My role feels misaligned"

Team, mission, or manager just changed materially.

Enter through: an employee-consultant conversation plus requisitions. An advisor helps you clarify what you want next; you apply competitively. Advisory starts when you ask, never before.

E · The At-Risk Employee

"I've heard my role may be at risk"

Restructure, elimination, or budget pressure looming.

Enter through: advisory plus priority requisition matching. Proactive support to find roles where your skills transfer, with expedited internal review.

F · The Direct Applicant

"There's an open job I want"

Knows the role, knows they're ready.

Enter through: the internal requisition listing. Apply inside the two-week internal window, interview competitively, and if selected, transition with a coordinated handoff.

Play · Pick the entrance

Why this matters: the entrances only help if you can match one to a real situation, including your own. Five colleagues; pick each one's best first door.

Go deeper

In practice

The entrances describe situations, never categories of people, and nobody is assigned one. Most staff will use two or three over a career: Explorer this year, Direct Applicant in three. The two advisory entrances (D and E) deserve a special word: the employee-consultant conversation is confidential, initiated by you, and nothing you share starts any action without your consent. For at-risk situations, entrance E adds priority matching and expedited review, which is the program working hardest exactly when someone needs it most.

Section 03 · The Process

Two tracks, both yours.

Everything in the portal runs on one of two tracks. Interest & Development is always on, private, and unhurried. Move starts when a requisition opens, and it is competitive. They live in the same place and feed each other.

Track 1 · Interest & Development, always on
  1. Build your profile. In your Oracle Talent Profile: update Career Preferences and tag both skills lists (Skills I have, Skills I'm developing) at honest levels. Private to you; rolls up only in aggregate.
  2. Set a Grow plan and flag your Careers of Interest. Both live in the Grow area: one to three development goals, plus the career roles you want suggestions about.
  3. Explore. Browse the Opportunity Marketplace: requisitions, career roles, pathways, and Gig postings. Save what interests you.
  4. Take a Gig, optionally. Defined scope, defined duration, real development. Never a trial for a transfer, never a guarantee.
  5. Ask for advisory support, optionally. An employee-consultant conversation, on your initiative. Nothing you share triggers action without your consent.
Track 2 · Move, event-driven
  1. A requisition opens and posts internally for two weeks before going external.
  2. You apply. The hiring manager sees what you choose to share. Performance data follows Vanderbilt policy; it is never surfaced casually.
  3. You interview, in the same process internal and external candidates get.
  4. Transition is coordinated. HR and both managers plan the handoff, typically two to four weeks. The releasing manager is a partner.
  5. You onboard fast, because your institutional knowledge comes with you.
  6. Your old role backfills through the same portal, so the releasing team gets the same first look you just used.

Go deeper

In practice

The disclosure question deserves a straight answer. You are never required to tell your manager the day you declare an interest, browse a role, or update your skills. You are expected to have a professional conversation once you are a serious candidate, before your manager learns about it from a requisition workflow. That timing is yours to choose, and choosing it well is part of competing respectfully. The other quiet rule: internal application never equals internal entitlement. The window gives you first look; the interview is still yours to win.

Section 04 · The Culture Shift

Let's talk about poaching.

Some managers will hear "internal mobility" and think "poaching." That reaction is predictable, and it has been measured: in the first large-scale study of talent hoarding, about three quarters of managers admitted to hoarding, it was strongest where managers are judged on team performance, and it measurably deters people from applying internally (Haegele, American Economic Review, 2024). If we do nothing, the average manager will quietly suppress the exact behavior this portal exists to enable. So the culture work is the work.

The reframe every manager needs

When a team member grows into a new role at Vanderbilt, that is a win for your leadership record. Every internal move is retention we would otherwise have lost to an outside offer, skills recombining where the university needs them, and a backfill that brings your own team fresh capability.

The response that builds the culture

"Thank you for telling me. Tell me what drew you to it. What can I do to help you compete well, and whatever the outcome, what should we plan for next in your development here?"

The response the culture cannot survive

"After everything I've invested in you?"

The first response builds a career at Vanderbilt. The second builds a resume for somewhere else.

Manager guardrails

No retaliation: blocking an application, downgrading a rating, or sidelining someone who applied is a serious violation. No surveillance: you are not notified of interest declarations; asking HR to work around that is off the table. Do coach: hold the "what's next for you here" conversation before a requisition holds it for you.

Play · Judge the response

Why this matters: the whole program lives or dies in the sixty seconds after a team member says "I've applied for something." Five manager responses; call each one.

Go deeper

In practice

Hoarding is rarely villainy; it is incentives. Haegele's research found it worst where managers are evaluated on team performance, teams are large, and talent is visible, which describes most of us on a normal Tuesday. So the rollout counters structure with structure, following Kotter's change model: urgency built from retention risk and vacancy costs, champion managers in every unit who volunteer to release talent and get celebrated for it, barriers removed through the internal-first window and structured handoffs, early anonymized "first mover" stories, and eventually a manager release rate on leader scorecards. Staff hold guardrails too: honest skill ratings, respectful competition, and a professional disclosure conversation once you're a serious candidate.

Section 05 · The Evidence

The case, in numbers.

This section is built to be borrowed: every claim is published, cited, and linked, ready for an executive briefing or a hallway debate. First, see how well your instincts match the research.

Play · Guess the number

Why this matters: a number you guessed at and missed sticks far better than one you were shown. Five findings; call each before the reveal.

Retention

70% vs 45%

Odds of staying for employees promoted within three years of hire, versus those who never moved. Lateral moves land at 62%. SHRM / LinkedIn, 32 million profiles.

Tenure

5.4 vs 2.9 years

Average tenure at high-mobility organizations versus low. Internal movers are 40% more likely to stay three years or more. Inop.ai benchmark synthesis; LinkedIn Global Talent Trends 2024.

Engagement

~60% higher

Engagement scores reported by employees after an internal move, plus a meta-analysis of 56 studies (N = 284,086) tying rotation to satisfaction, commitment, and lower burnout. Inop.ai; Mlekus & Maier.

Speed

20 to 25% faster

How much sooner internal hires reach full competency; internal-first hiring also cuts time-to-hire by roughly 10 to 20 days. LinkedIn Workplace Learning Report; Bersin Factbook.

Cost

Up to 3 to 5x

Full cost of hiring externally once time and lost productivity count; direct costs alone run 18 to 20% higher. Bersin; SHRM 2024 benchmarking.

Quality

Internal > external

Cornell ILR: internal hires outperform external hires and stay longer, and the best internal hires are the likeliest to remain. Rotation-heavy firms are also disproportionately the innovative ones. Cornell ILR; Eriksson & Ortega.

Quick check: These outcomes are real, and they still won't happen at Vanderbilt automatically. Which finding explains why?

Go deeper

In practice

The one-sentence pitch, ready for an executive: "The Transfer Portal is Vanderbilt's staff-facing internal talent marketplace, a private, Oracle-powered experience where employees see internal opportunities first, signal interest safely, and compete for stretch work or new roles, while leaders see aggregated readiness and risk through Workforce Intelligence." Two more numbers for the skeptic: 93% of employees stay longer at organizations that invest in their careers (LinkedIn Workplace Learning Report), and industry internal-hire rates have slumped to about 24% from a 2020 peak near 40% (AMS), which is the gap Vanderbilt gets to win in.
